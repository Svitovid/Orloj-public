# Pansophia Atlas v0 — Vision

## Purpose

Pansophia Atlas is the spatial and civilizational interface of Pansophia Institute.

It answers four questions:

- **WHEN?** — Chronos / Orloj
- **WHERE?** — geography and place
- **WHAT?** — society, institutions, technology, economy, culture, consciousness
- **WHY?** — competing explanations and later Sofilology synthesis

The Atlas is not only a map. It is a **living atlas of man and civilization**: a way to move through time, place and layers of human life without flattening them into one explanation.

## Product architecture

Two coordinated editions are planned:

1. **Atlas Professional** — deeper research interface, citations, sources, multiple interpretive layers, data-rich historical slices.
2. **Atlas Schola** — simplified educational edition for pupils and general audiences, story-led, visual, map-first and activity-friendly.

Both should share the same underlying knowledge model.

## Core interaction

A user chooses:

1. a **time** or period,
2. a **place** or region,
3. one or more **civilizational layers**,
4. optionally an **interpretive model**.

The interface then shows how the selected layers interact.

Example:

**Prague 1618**

- Society
- Institutions
- Religion
- Economy
- Media / print
- Technology
- Power
- Culture
- Consciousness

The Atlas should make it possible to compare the same place across time, or the same year across multiple places.

## Civilizational layers

Initial layer set:

- **Society** — family, class, community, status, demography, trust
- **Institutions** — state, church, university, corporation, platform, protocol
- **Technology** — tools, energy, transport, communication, computation
- **Economy** — money, trade, work, ownership, finance
- **Media** — oral culture, writing, print, broadcast, platforms, AI
- **Power** — military, political, financial, cultural, informational power
- **Culture** — art, aesthetics, myth, fashion, literature, ritual
- **Sacrum** — religion, transcendence, sacred order
- **Consciousness** — identity, attention, selfhood, relation to world and cosmos
- **Body / Life** — health, food, work, sleep, reproduction, everyday bodily life
- **Environment** — climate, landscape, city, infrastructure, resources
- **Cosmos** — calendars, astronomy, astrology as optional traditional layer

These are lenses, not isolated silos.

## Epistemic separation

The Atlas must clearly distinguish:

- observed / documented historical facts,
- scholarly interpretations,
- traditional or theological interpretations,
- hypotheses,
- speculative or symbolic overlays.

Interpretive layers may coexist, but they must not be visually confused with the empirical base layer.

## First vertical slice

The first prototype should not attempt the whole world.

Use one historical slice to test the interface:

**Central Europe / Prague / 1618**

Why:

- strong connection to Czech and Moravian history,
- institutions, religion, media, money and power are all visible,
- natural link to Comenius and the early modern transformation,
- suitable later for school use.

The prototype is a product-design test, not a final historical dataset.

## Visual language

Atlas should feel related to the Orloj Reader but not identical.

Shared qualities:

- luminous color,
- paper and glass,
- rare-book tactility,
- restrained liquid-glass surfaces,
- photographic depth,
- wide-gamut / 4K-first design,
- strong typography,
- clean white as a material and contrast.

Atlas-specific qualities:

- cartographic greens, ultramarine and parchment,
- layered translucent map surfaces,
- map-room / Renaissance studiolo atmosphere,
- subtle topographic and manuscript textures,
- visible relationships between layers rather than dashboard density.

## Navigation model

Primary controls:

- time rail,
- map / place selector,
- layer dock,
- narrative / evidence drawer,
- compare mode.

The user should always know:

- where they are,
- when they are,
- which layers are active,
- whether they are looking at evidence or interpretation.

## Relationship to Orloj

Orloj and Atlas share time.

- Orloj begins from **a day / cycle / sky** and opens outward.
- Atlas begins from **a place / civilization / historical slice** and opens across time.

Long-term principle:

> one knowledge graph, multiple interfaces.
